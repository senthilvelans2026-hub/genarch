Writing a good prompt

The plan and camera already fix the room's shape. Your words decide everything else. Mention these five things; anything you leave out, we fill in for you.

RoomWhat the space is used for: bedroom, kitchen, home office.Left out: read from the plan and the furniture in it.
TimeTime of day and light: morning sun, golden hour, night with lamps on.Left out: soft natural daylight.
StyleThe design language: Japandi, modern Indian, industrial.Left out: clean contemporary.
MaterialsFloor, walls, wood, stone, fabric, colours.Left out: chosen to suit the style.
MoodHow it should feel: cosy, airy, calm, dramatic.Left out: inviting and balanced.
Example
Master bedroom, golden hour with warm low sun through the window. Japandi style. Light oak floor, off-white plaster walls, linen bedding in sand tones. Calm and cosy.

Check what goes into the 3D model

The model is built from this picture and its labels. Walls, doors, windows and the built-ins marked solid are built and textured from the picture; movable furniture marked dashed becomes a simple marker. Anything not labelled is not built.

Want something changed? Change the labels and render again — each picture keeps its own labels and its own 3D model, so the textures always match what is built.

Pick the room to render

The renders work best on one room, or a small group of rooms. A bigger area spreads the model's attention over walls and furniture your camera will never see, and small details in your room get missed. Drag a box around the room you want, then move it or pull its edges and corners to adjust.
